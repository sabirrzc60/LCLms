How Equations Anchor Every Leaving Cert Chemistry Question

In Leaving Certificate Chemistry, a balanced equation is rarely an isolated exercise—it is the mole ledger behind volumetric titration calculations, heats of reaction and combustion, $K_c$ equilibrium expressions, organic reactions, nuclear decay, and water analysis.

Stage 1 • Beginner

Formulas, Ions & States

Write valid ionic and covalent formulas first, recognize diatomics (H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂), and apply state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq) or reversible arrows ⇌.

Stage 2 • Intermediate

Atoms, Half-Equations & Charge

Balance coefficients by inspection without changing subscripts, balance half-equations using H₂O, H⁺, and e⁻, and conserve mass (A) and charge (Z) in nuclear decay.

Stage 3 • H1 Achiever

SEC Mole Ratios & Precision

Extract exact mole ratios for titrations, thermochemistry ($\Delta H$), and $K_c$ powers; avoid common sign, factor-of-two, and spectator-ion traps under exam time pressure.

Lab B • Redox Half-Equations

Half-Equation Electron Balancing & Redox Combiner

In acidic solution (typical for LC volumetric redox titrations), balance each half-equation in order: (1) Main atoms → (2) O with H₂O → (3) H with H⁺ → (4) Charge with e⁻.

Guided Example: Manganate(VII) + Iron(II) in Acidic Solution
Reduction half: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O
Oxidation half: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + 1e⁻  (multiply by 5 so electrons match: 5Fe²⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + 5e⁻)
Overall redox: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O  [Mole ratio n(MnO₄⁻) : n(Fe²⁺) = 1 : 5]
Interactive Electron Multiplier Check: Oxalate / Ethanedioate & Manganate(VII)

The reduction half uses 5e⁻ per MnO₄⁻, while the oxidation half C₂O₄²⁻ → 2CO₂ + 2e⁻ releases 2e⁻ per C₂O₄²⁻. To cancel electrons cleanly (LCM of 5 and 2 is 10), what multiplier must you apply to MnO₄⁻ and what multiplier to C₂O₄²⁻?
