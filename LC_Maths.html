Leaving Certificate Mathematics (Higher Level) Exam Architecture

Higher Level Mathematics is assessed across two written papers totaling 600 marks: Paper 1 (300 marks, 2h 30m) and Paper 2 (300 marks, 2h 30m). Each paper contains Section A (Concepts & Skills, 150m) and Section B (Contexts & Applications, 150m). In addition, under Irish CAO rules, students achieving a H6 or higher in Higher Level Mathematics receive a 25-point bonus toward CAO points calculations (up to 625 maximum).

Paper 1 • 300 Marks (2h 30m)

Algebra, Complex Numbers, Sequences & Calculus

  • Section A — Concepts & Skills (150m): Core manipulation, factor/remainder theorems, indices & logs, Argand/polar complex numbers, series/induction, differentiation & integration rules.
  • Section B — Contexts & Applications (150m): Multi-step modelling, financial maths (amortisation/present value), De Moivre applications, rates of change, optimisation, and area between curves.
Paper 2 • 300 Marks (2h 30m)

Co-ordinate & Synthetic Geometry, Trig, Probability & Stats

  • Section A — Concepts & Skills (150m): Line & circle geometry, geometric proofs/constructions, radian trigonometry & identities, counting/probability rules, summary statistics & z-scores.
  • Section B — Contexts & Applications (150m): Tangency/locus problems, 3D/non-right trigonometry, conditional/expected/binomial probability, confidence intervals & hypothesis testing conclusions in context.

Pacing & H1 Execution Guide

Time Budgeting (~0.5 min/mark)

Each paper gives 150 minutes for 300 marks (~70–75 min Section A, ~70–75 min Section B, leaving 5–10 min review). If a part stalls for 3–4 minutes, mark it and move on to bank sure marks.

Formulae & Tables + Calculator Mode

Know where every formula sits in the Formulae and Tables booklet (series, De Moivre, calculus, line/circle, trig identities, normal/stats). Check RAD vs. DEG before every trig or complex polar calculation.

Partial Credit & Exact Working

SEC marking schemes award High, Mid, and Low partial credit for valid intermediate steps. Always write the formula, substitute clearly, keep exact surds/fractions/π until the final rounded line, and state units.

Lab 1 • Paper 1 Algebra & Calculus

Quadratic Discriminant, Roots & Turning Point Explorer

For a quadratic f(x) = ax² + bx + c (with a ≠ 0), the discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac determines root nature, while xv = −b / (2a) gives the turning point (vertex) and derivative f′(x) = 2ax + b.

Lab 2 • Paper 1 Complex Numbers

Modulus-Argument (Polar Form) & De Moivre’s Theorem Solver

Converts z = x + iy into polar form r(cos θ + i sin θ) where r = √(x² + y²) and principal argument θ ∈ (−π, π], then computes zn = rn(cos nθ + i sin nθ).

Lab 3 • Paper 2 Co-ordinate Geometry

Line Segment & Circle Equation / Tangency Analyzer

Analyses two points A(x1, y1) and B(x2, y2) (slope, midpoint, distance) and checks a circle centred at (h, k) with radius r.

Lab 4 • Paper 2 Inferential Statistics

Normal Distribution z-Score & Sample Mean Standard Error Helper

Computes z = (x − μ) / σ for an individual observation and SE = σ / √n plus zx̄ = (x̄ − μ) / (σ / √n) for a sample of size n.
